Facilitator preparation and learning outcomes

Bring a device, internet access and private notes. Arrange groups of four for the clue puzzle and pairs for reflection. Test links beforehand and prepare the fictional example. For large rooms, use seated table discussions; online, use pair breakout rooms.

Participants practise identifying three source types, checking their audiences, distinguishing fact from inference, and choosing a practical privacy change. This is a first pass, with the directory supporting further checks afterwards.

No exposure leaderboard. Do not collect worksheets, project personal findings or search colleagues. If someone is uncomfortable, offer the fictional profile or a private break. Identify an internal contact for exposed work information.

What a public search cannot tell you

Search engines do not cover every account, link, archive or breach. A result might be outdated or belong to someone else. Confirm identity and recency before drawing conclusions. Nothing found is not proof of invisibility.

Account exports, purchase history, ad preferences and location history show what a provider holds. They do not by themselves show what strangers can access. Review those privately as optional homework.

01 / Choose your sources

Unused services can be marked N/A. Unchecked is a useful answer too.

02 / Understand a finding

Public, followers-only and link access are different audiences. A signed-out window can help check visibility; it does not make you anonymous.

03 / Change something

Keep information you intentionally want visible. If someone else publishes it, ask for a targeted change. Google search removal does not delete the source page.

04 / Keep going

Private exit check

Can I name three source types I checked? Can I explain a fact and an inference? Have I completed a change or marked it pending? Have I dated two next steps?
