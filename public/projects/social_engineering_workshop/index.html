02 / A SHORT READ

When the assistant becomes the target.

06–12 min
The security brief2025 case · AI security

A crafted email could make Copilot leak internal data

A vulnerability called EchoLeak showed how an ordinary-looking email could carry malicious instructions for Microsoft 365 Copilot. Under certain conditions, the assistant could be steered into disclosing limited internal information already accessible to the user.

Microsoft fixed the flaw, tracked as CVE-2025-32711. The case illustrates a risk when an assistant processes outside content: material it should read as data can instead influence its behaviour.

Short paraphrased case summary, not a report of a confirmed customer breach. Source: Microsoft Security, 17 December 2025 ↗

Discuss: who or what is being influenced? At what point is outside content trusted?

Human persuasion and prompt injection are different mechanisms. Both invite us to examine where trust is granted.

03 / WHAT MAKES A REQUEST PERSUASIVE?

Eight elements to recognise.

12–20 min

Open a few examples. Which would matter in your situation? Add others.

Authority

“I’m calling from IT.” A claimed role makes a request seem legitimate.

Urgency

“We need this now.” Time pressure reduces the opportunity to check.

Scarcity

“Last place available.” Fear of missing out makes delay feel costly.

Social proof

“Everyone else has done it.” Other people’s supposed actions normalise a request.

Familiarity and liking

A shared interest, familiar name or friendly manner makes contact feel expected.

Reciprocity

“I helped you earlier.” A favour can create pressure to return it.

Commitment and consistency

A small initial agreement makes a larger follow-up feel like the next step.

Emotional pull

Fear, excitement or the wish to help can compete with careful checking.

In pairs: pick two elements. When are they useful in ordinary life, and when could they be exploited?

Discussion prompts, not a formula for predicting behaviour. Several draw on Cialdini’s persuasion principles; urgency and emotional pressure are added here.

05 / TEAMS OF 2–3

Build the story. Then challenge it.

32–54 min

Pick a fictional or volunteered target. Describe the scenario; do not send messages or make calls.

3 min / choose6 min / create8 min / challenge5 min / role-play and revise

Your scenario

Under what conditions could it work?

Consider timing, workload, an expected interaction, believable information and a process that allows the request.

Under what conditions would it fail?

Consider a separate verification channel, access limits, a second approval, or feeling comfortable saying “let me check”.

One person plays the social engineer; one plays the target; the third observes. Swap roles. Change one condition and try the scene again.

In a pair, discuss the observer’s view afterwards. For an AI scenario, map the content, permissions and trust boundary instead of attributing human psychology to the model.
